# Privacy Policy — The Bridge Adv Runs

*Last updated: [DATE] · Draft version*

This policy explains what personal information "The Bridge Adv Runs" (also called "Bridge Runs," "the app") collects, why we collect it, and what choices you have. We wrote it in plain language for our community. If anything here is unclear, please reach out using the contact details at the bottom.

The app is a simple tool for organizing a weekly recreational basketball run connected to The Bridge church in Markham, Ontario. It is currently built and operated by an individual developer (Daniel, "the Developer") for community use.

## What information we collect

We only collect what we need to run the check-in and community features:

- **Your name** — so hosts and other players can recognize you on the roster.
- **Your email address** — used to sign you in (we send a "magic link" instead of a password) and to send you app-related messages.
- **Your phone number** — optional, used by the host to reach you about the run.
- **Profile photo** — optional, if you choose to add one.
- **Chat messages and any photos you share** — in the shared group chat and in 1:1 messages to the host.
- **Check-in and attendance records** — which runs you signed up for, whether you got a floor spot or were on the waitlist, and your attendance history.
- **Role and approval information** — your role within the community and records related to the approval flow for new players.
- **Basic technical/security information** — the app may keep sign-in activity needed to keep your account secure and the app running.

We do **not** collect payment information, and we don't run advertising or sell data.

**Note on the physical-activity waiver:** The waiver that covers the risks of playing basketball is handled separately on the Church's ChurchCenter system, not inside this app. That waiver has its own terms.

---

## Why we collect it and our legal basis

We collect this information to:

- Sign you in securely and manage your account.
- Let you check in for the run and manage the limited floor spots and waitlist.
- Show rosters and attendance to hosts and admins.
- Let you chat with the group and message the host.
- Post announcements and manage new-player approvals and invites.

Our basis for handling your information is **your consent**, given when you create an account and use the app, plus what is reasonably necessary to provide the service you signed up for. Under Canadian privacy law (including PIPEDA, which is likely to apply if the app is ever licensed or sold commercially), we aim to collect only what we need, use it only for the reasons above, and keep it secure.

You can withdraw consent at any time by asking us to delete your account (see "Your rights" below). Some features won't work without the basic information (for example, we can't sign you in without an email).

---

## Who can see your information

- **Hosts and admins** can see rosters, check-in status, attendance history, player profiles, roles, and messages sent to the host. This is necessary to run the group.
- **Other players** can see your name, profile photo (if you add one), and anything you post in the shared group chat.
- **1:1 messages to the host** are visible to the host/admins, not to the wider group.
- The Developer (and, in future, the Church as operator) can access stored data as needed to operate, support, and secure the app.

Please treat the group chat as semi-public within the community. Don't share anything there you wouldn't want other players to see.

---

## How your information is stored and protected

- The app runs on Node.js with a SQLite database.
- It is currently self-hosted on the Developer's personal computer and made available over an encrypted HTTPS connection (via Tailscale Funnel).
- Sign-in uses email "magic links" rather than stored passwords.
- Access to the underlying data is limited to the operator.

We take reasonable steps to protect your information, but no online service can promise perfect security. If the app is handed over to the Church, hosting and data storage would move to the Church's own infrastructure, and the Church would be responsible for securing it from that point.
